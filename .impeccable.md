## Design Context

### Users
- **Primary: hackathon judges** (PayPal product and developer people). They open the hosted demo on a laptop, in daytime, with no account, and give it about two minutes. They must understand without help what the agent did, why the guard allowed or blocked it, and what proof the merchant ends up holding.
- **Personas shown in the demo**:
  - a buyer who delegates a purchase to an AI agent under a written mandate (budget, merchant, duration, approval threshold);
  - a merchant who receives a dispute and answers it with the sealed receipt.
- The UI is in English and stays in PayPal sandbox only.

### Brand Personality
- Three words: **notarial, exact, calm**.
- Emotional goal: confidence. The judge should think "this would hold up in front of a bank". The sealed receipt is treated like an official deed: dated, numbered, signed, verifiable.
- Voice: plain, factual sentences. The interface states what happened ("Blocked: merchant not in mandate"), never hypes it. No exclamation marks, no "magic".

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Light, paper-like theme only**: warm off-white surfaces, ink-coloured text, tinted neutrals (never pure black or white). It must also read well in a screen recording.
- **Own identity**: Mandat has its own name, type and colour. PayPal appears only where it really acts (the official approval step, "PayPal sandbox" labels, order and dispute IDs). Do not imitate PayPal's blues or branding.
- References in spirit: printed legal and financial documents (deeds, bank statements, stamped receipts), clear typesetting, ruled lines, a seal as the one strong graphic mark.
- **Anti-reference: the generic AI demo**: no purple or blue gradients, no dark neon, no glowing accents, no chat window at the centre of the screen. The agent's reasoning is shown as a log of decisions, not as a chatbot.
- Hashes and signatures appear where they prove something (receipt, verify page), set small and secondary, never as decoration.

### Design Principles
1. **The receipt is the hero.** Every screen leads towards the proof: what the mandate allowed, what was decided, what PayPal recorded.
2. **Decisions read like a ledger.** A chronological, numbered log with a clear verdict per line (allowed, needs approval, blocked) and the rule that produced it.
3. **Authority is visible.** It is always clear who decided: the agent proposes, the guard decides, the human approves, PayPal records.
4. **Honest states.** Anything simulated or replayed is labelled as such. Waiting on PayPal is shown as waiting, with the expected delay (disputes take about 3 minutes to arrive).
5. **Restraint.** One accent colour used rarely (verdicts, the seal), generous whitespace, few type sizes with strong contrast. WCAG AA minimum and reduced-motion respected.
